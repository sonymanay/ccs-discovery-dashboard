Content Maintenance — what triggers updates & what AI can auto-do

Maintenance is the most ambiguous pillar in most content programs. This table makes triggers explicit, names the signal source (Health vs Performance vs Feedback), and calls out where AI can safely auto-update vs where humans must own the change.

How Health & Performance connect to Maintenance

  • Health produces the passive backlog: freshness expirations, inventory drift, quality-score decay, owner churn.
  • Performance produces the active backlog: things people are actually using badly — search failures, citation errors, deflection drops, negative feedback.
  • Both feed a connected Maintenance queue with the diagnostic signal attached, so the owner sees why the work is needed before opening the editor.
  • Updated content goes back through the Governance Gate — never published directly.

Governance — where should it sit?

Governance is not a single stage — it's a cross-cutting plane with three placements, in priority order:

  1. Pre-publish gate (primary, highest leverage): automated checks (metadata, brand, legal, SEO, a11y, PII, provenance, dedup) + targeted human review. Single gate replaces fragmented per-surface review.
  2. Upstream standards in Creation: templates, voice rules, schema, AI-readiness baked into the authoring surface so problems are avoided, not corrected.
  3. Ongoing audits on live content: drift detection, periodic re-checks. Updated content re-enters the gate on every significant change.

Trust & Safety (PII, hallucination detection, provenance) is treated as a separate plane because the AI Creation path needs heavier, real-time enforcement than the human path.
